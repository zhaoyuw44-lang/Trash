# Polymer Physics — free / open-access material package

About **Rubinstein & Colby, _Polymer Physics_ (Oxford University Press, 2003)**: this book is
still in copyright and OUP does **not** distribute it as a free PDF. The authors' own site
(http://polymerphysics.net/) and Ralph Colby's Penn State page list the book but host no
full text, and there is no open-access edition, no Internet Archive lending copy, and no
author-posted manuscript. Anything advertised online as a "free PDF of Rubinstein & Colby"
is an unauthorized scan. So this folder contains the legitimate free equivalents instead —
material that covers the same ground and is explicitly published for free by its authors or
institutions.

## What is here

| File | What it is | License / source |
|---|---|---|
| `Steimel_Polymers_and_Composite_Materials_2022_CC-BY-NC-ND.pdf` | A complete 257-page polymer physics textbook. Covers chain models and scaling laws, Flory–Huggins, viscosity, osmometry, SEC/GPC, light scattering, block-copolymer self-assembly, semi-crystalline and amorphous polymers, polymer dynamics (entanglement, reptation), rubber elasticity, viscoelasticity, composites, yielding. Chapter bookmarks added. | CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, Pacific Open Texts, University of the Pacific |
| `MIT_3.063_Polymer_Physics_Lecture_Notes.pdf` | MIT 3.063 *Polymer Physics* (Prof. Edwin "Ned" Thomas), all 22 lectures merged into one 519-page PDF with a bookmark per lecture. The closest free counterpart to a Rubinstein-style course: ideal chains, Flory–Huggins, Zimm plots, Tg, reptation, Flory–Rehner gels, block copolymers, mechanical properties. | MIT OpenCourseWare, CC BY-NC-SA |
| `MIT_3.063_Polymer_Physics_Problem_Sets.pdf` | The five problem sets from the same MIT course, for practice. | MIT OpenCourseWare, CC BY-NC-SA |
| `Rubinstein_Polymer_Physics_Lectures_2-3_author_slides.pdf` | Michael Rubinstein's **own** 58-slide lecture set written for the Boulder Summer School: real chains, Flory theory, thermodynamics of mixtures, polymer solutions. Same notation and scaling arguments as the book. | Posted publicly by the author via the Boulder School (Yale/CU Boulder) |
| `McLeish_Polymer_Dynamics_Boulder2012_slides.pdf` | Tom McLeish's 55-slide course on polymer dynamics: Rouse model, entanglement, reptation, tube models — the material of the book's Part 4. | Posted publicly via the Boulder School |
| `Rubinstein_Colby_Oxford_TableOfContents.pdf` | The book's actual table of contents, so you can map each chapter onto the substitutes above. | Posted by the authors on polymerphysics.net |

## Suggested mapping onto the real book

- **Part 1 — single chain conformations (Ch. 2–4)**: Rubinstein slides 2&3, then Steimel Ch. 1 & 3, MIT lectures 1–2.
- **Part 2 — thermodynamics of solutions and melts (Ch. 5–9)**: Steimel Ch. 4–8, MIT lectures 3–6.
- **Part 3 — networks and gels (Ch. 10–12)**: Steimel Ch. 10 & 12, MIT lecture 9 (Flory–Rehner).
- **Part 4 — dynamics (Ch. 13–16)**: McLeish slides, Steimel Ch. 11 & 13, MIT lectures 7–8.

## Getting the actual book

- Buy or rent: Oxford University Press (ISBN 9780198520597). The OUP Academic platform
  (https://academic.oup.com/book/54754) sells institutional and individual online access.
- Library: most university libraries carry it; check your institution's OUP Academic or
  ProQuest/EBSCO subscription, which usually includes the full online text.
- Note there is an errata sheet for the 2003 first printing; the 2004–2006 reprints already
  contain the corrections.
